# Coach Reference Sheet: Copyright Act 1987 (Act 332) and Facebook Terms

## 1. Subsistence: does copyright exist in the photograph?

**Quoted**
- s.3 "artistic work" means "(a) a graphic work, photograph, sculpture or collage, irrespective of artistic quality;"
- s.3 "photograph" means "a recording of light or other radiation on any medium on which an image is produced or from which an image may by any means be produced, and which is not part of a film;"
- s.7(1) "... the following works shall be eligible for copyright: ... (c) artistic works;"
- s.7(2) "Works shall be protected irrespective of their quality and the purpose for which they were created."
- s.7(2A) "Copyright protection shall not extend to any idea, procedure, method of operation or mathematical concept as such."
- s.7(3) "A literary, musical or artistic work shall not be eligible for copyright unless— (a) sufficient effort has been expended to make the work original in character; and (b) the work has been written down, recorded or otherwise reduced to material form."
- s.10(1) "Copyright shall subsist in every work eligible for copyright of which the author ... is, at the time when the work is made, a qualified person."
- s.10(3) "Notwithstanding subsections (1) and (2), copyright shall subsist, subject to this Act, in every work eligible for copyright if the work is made in Malaysia."
- s.3 "qualified person" means, for an individual, "a person who is a citizen of, or a permanent resident in, Malaysia".
- s.26A is headed "Voluntary notification of copyright" (registration is not a condition of protection).

**Analysis**
- "Just snapping" is not the test. Quality is irrelevant (s.3, s.7(2)). The test is s.7(3): "sufficient effort ... to make the work original in character", plus material form (a saved phone photo). Choices like subject, framing and timing are the effort.
- "No effort at all is needed" and "only professional photos count" are both wrong. Ignoring s.7(3)(a) is incomplete.
- No registration is needed.
- **Possible extra point:** the case says Dave and Tom live in Singapore. Dave may not be a "qualified person" (s.10(1)), but the photos were made in Malaysia (Mulu, Sarawak), so s.10(3) applies. This is not in the current six-point checklist.

## 2. Ownership and authorship

**Quoted**
- s.3 "author", in relation to photographs, "means the person by whom the arrangements for the taking of the photograph were undertaken;"
- s.26(1) "Copyright conferred by section 10 shall vest initially in the author."
- s.26(2) transfers copyright to a commissioner or employer where the work is commissioned by a non-employer or made in the course of employment, "subject to any agreement between the parties".

**Analysis**
- Dave took his own photos on holiday, so he is the author and first owner. Tom's photos are Tom's. The s.26(2) employer and commissioner rule does not apply.

## 3. Facebook posting is not a waiver

**Quoted (Act)**
- s.27(3) "No assignment of copyright and no licence to do an act the doing of which is controlled by copyright shall have effect unless it is in writing."
- s.3 "communication to the public" means "the transmission of a work or performance through wire or wireless means to the public, including the making available of a work or performance to the public in such a way that members of the public may access the work or performance from a place and at a time individually chosen by them;"

**Facebook Terms s.3.3 (English rendering of the Malay page, to be checked)**
- Users keep ownership of the intellectual property rights (such as copyright) in content they create and share on Facebook.
- By sharing or uploading IP-protected content on or in connection with Facebook's Products, the user gives Facebook a non-exclusive, transferable, sub-licensable, royalty-free, worldwide licence to host, use, distribute, modify, run, copy, publicly perform or display it.
- The licence ends when the content is deleted from Facebook's systems, except where others have used the content under the licence and have not deleted it.

**Analysis**
- Posting is not an assignment or waiver: ownership stays with Dave, and any assignment or licence needs writing (s.27(3)).
- The licence described is granted to Facebook. It does not purport to license other users such as Greg (confirm against the full Terms).
- Being publicly visible is not consent to copying. Dave keeps the exclusive rights in s.13(1).

## 4. Infringement by Greg

**Quoted**
- s.13(1) "Copyright in a literary, musical or artistic work ... shall be the exclusive right to control in Malaysia— (a) the reproduction in any material form; (aa) the communication to the public; (b) the performance, showing or playing to the public; ... (e) the distribution of copies to the public by sale or other transfer of ownership; and (f) the commercial rental to the public, of the whole work or a substantial part thereof ..."
- s.36(1) "Copyright is infringed by any person who does, or causes any other person to do, without the licence of the owner of the copyright, an act the doing of which is controlled by copyright under this Act."
- s.3 "reproduction" means "the making of one or more copies of a work in any form or version".
- s.13(2)(a) the fair dealing exception covers "research, private study, criticism, review or the reporting of news or current events", and needs an acknowledgement of title and authorship.

**Analysis**
- Greg copied several of Dave's photos and submitted them to the competition without Dave's licence. That is an act controlled by copyright (s.13(1)(a)), and possibly "causes any other person to do" further acts such as the organiser's or newspaper's use (s.36(1)).
- Fair dealing does not cover entering a competition for a prize.
- Not knowing it was infringing does not stop it being infringement. It only affects the remedies (s.37(6)).

## 5. Moral rights and false attribution

**Quoted**
- s.25(2)(a) "... no person may, without the consent of the author ... do or authorize the doing of any of the following acts: (a) the presentation of the work, by any means whatsoever, without identifying the author or under a name other than that of the author;"
- s.25(5) "Any contravention or threatened contravention of this section in respect of a work shall be actionable at the suit of the author of the work ... as a breach of statutory duty."
- s.25(7) "Where in an action brought under this section a contravention of the restrictions imposed by this section is proved or admitted, the court may order the offender to publish such correction in such manner as the court may direct."
- s.25(8) "Nothing in this section shall derogate from any right of action or other remedy (whether civil or criminal) in proceedings instituted otherwise than by virtue of this section ..."

**Analysis**
- Greg presenting Dave's photo as his own (collecting the prize, appearing in the newspaper as the winner) engages s.25(2)(a). The right belongs to Dave as author (s.25(4) says it can be exercised even where the copyright is not held by the author).
- A court-ordered published correction (s.25(7)) is a direct answer to Dave's wish for public acknowledgement.

## 6. Remedies

**Quoted: civil (s.37)**
- s.37(1) "... the court may grant the following types of relief: (a) an order for injunction; (b) damages; (c) an account of profits; (d) statutory damages of not more than twenty-five thousand ringgit for each work, but not more than five hundred thousand ringgit in the aggregate; or (e) any other order as the court deems fit."
- s.37(3) with an award of damages, "the court may also make an order under paragraph (1)(c) for an account of any profits attributable to the infringement ... that have not been taken into account in computing the damages."
- s.37(4) "Except as provided in subsection (3), the types of relief referred to in paragraphs (1)(b), (c) and (d) are mutually exclusive."
- s.37(6) if the defendant "was not aware, and had no reasonable grounds for suspecting, that the act was an infringement", the plaintiff "shall not be entitled ... to any damages ... but shall be entitled to an account of profits or statutory damages".

**Quoted: criminal (Part VI and VII)**
- s.41(1) "Any person who during the subsistence of copyright in a work ... (c) distributes infringing copies; ... (k) provides or shares access to an online location of any works or copies of works to any other person without authority, shall, unless he is able to prove that he had acted in good faith and had no reasonable grounds for supposing that copyright ... would or might thereby be infringed, be guilty of an offence ..."
- Penalty for (a) to (f): "a fine of not less than two thousand ringgit and not more than twenty thousand ringgit for each infringing copy, or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding five years or to both". For (k): a fine not exceeding RM250,000 or imprisonment not exceeding five years or both.
- s.50(1) an Assistant Controller or police officer not below the rank of Inspector "shall have the power to investigate the commission of any offence under this Act".
- s.50A(1) "Any Assistant Controller may arrest without warrant any person whom he reasonably believes has committed or is attempting to commit seizable offence under this Act ..."
- s.53 "No prosecution for any offence under this Act shall be instituted except by or with the consent in writing of the Public Prosecutor."
- s.54(2) on conviction or not, the court may order seized infringing copies "delivered up to the first owner of the copyright".

**Analysis**
- **Civil route (how Dave actually gets money):** injunction, damages, account of profits (Greg's benefit, arguably including the RM5,000 prize) or statutory damages (per work, so several photos matters). Damages and an account are alternatives except as s.37(3) allows. Nothing is automatic: Dave must sue, or credibly threaten to. A demand letter and negotiation are the usual first steps (that is practice, not in the Act). A published correction is available for the moral-rights breach (s.25(7)).
- **Criminal route:** the Act does create offences, so "infringement is always civil" is wrong. But investigation, arrest and prosecution are for the authorities (s.50, s.50A, s.53). Dave cannot direct an arrest or a prosecution. A criminal fine goes to the state and does not hand the prize money to Dave. Whether Greg's single competition entry would fall within s.41(1)(c) or (k), given the good-faith defence, is arguable and is for the lecturer to say.
- **Statements to mark as wrong:** "the prize money transfers automatically", or "the police will arrest Greg on Dave's say-so". **Statements to mark as overstated:** "infringement is a crime" said as a certainty.
